## Two books.

Ezra and Nehemiah were originally one book. Ezra tells how they rebuilt the temple and repurposed the people's devotion. Nehemiah tells how they built the city walls to protect themselves.

## Before the rebrand, we read Ezra.

I learned this lesson from Roger Patterson and Aaron Senseman. We were basically doing a rebrand. New name, new faces, a new temple of sorts.

Before any of it launched, Roger asked everyone to read Ezra and Nehemiah.

That was the practice. Before you take on a new venture, take account. Find out where along the path you actually are, and your pace.

## The mandate.

Looking at Ezra, God's people are in exile, the temple is rubble, and King Cyrus issues a decree: go home and rebuild the temple, the house of God.

A call to adventure. A mandate. A vision, and quasi a re-vision.

Vision is clarity from God on what to build. We are made in His image. He is a creator, so we are creators. The vision is one end: the goal, where we're headed.

And that brings us to the beginning. Before you can build anything toward said vision, you have to know where you're at.

So step zero: take account.

## Count everything.

Ezra opens with 2 chapters of inventory.

30 gold dishes. 1,000 silver dishes. 29 knives. 5,400 vessels of gold and silver in all. Then the people, family by family: 40k+ total. Then the singers, the gatekeepers, the servants. Then the animals. Then the gold and silver.

When they finished the temple, they counted the offerings. When Ezra arrived decades later, he weighed out the silver and gold before the journey and weighed it all again when he arrived. Scripture records it like an audit:

> "By number and by weight of every one: and all the weight was written at that time."
> Ezra 8:34

Feels diff than bookkeeping. They were marking the starting point.

> "The map is not the territory."
> Alfred Korzybski

The map is never the territory. But they were trying to get their map as close to the territory as possible.

> "For which of you, intending to build a tower, sitteth not down first, and counteth the cost?"
> Luke 14:28

## The delta is the work.

The vision gives you the end state. The count gives you the starting point. The delta between them is the work.

Purpose on one side. Gifts and resources on the other. What you're responsible for is everything in between.

You have to take your gifts, your resources, and the things you're responsible for, and turn them into the product that is the vision. Kind of like an old alchemist. Lead into gold. Rubble into a temple.

Study brings clarity to what you misunderstand.\
Practice closes the gap between the starting point and the goal.\
Both get articulated and iterated through time.

Most people want to skip the work. They want the vision, they want the resources, and they want the two to find resolution on their own. That's just not how it is.

You have to build the temple. You have to break your back. You have to hold the picture of the finished temple and step toward it. The delta doesn't shrink on its own. It shrinks because you work toward it.

There is no limit to what can be accomplished once you add the element of **TIME**.

Even in Ezra, the people got a little too precious about time. The foundation was laid, opposition showed up, and the work sat for about 16 years. The prophet Haggai named their excuse:

> "This people say, The time is not come, the time that the LORD's house should be built."
> Haggai 1:2

"It's taking too long." Too long according to who?

Time isn't the enemy of the vision. Time is the furnace.

## The charge.

So, my heuristic: **Take Account, Then Take Action.**

Your ambition, your dream, your mandate (remember, the vision is a gift from God) is simply the end point. What's in your hands is the starting point.

## What do I do w my hands?

The work isn't separate from your vision or what's in your hands. The work is what you do with the resources in your hands.
